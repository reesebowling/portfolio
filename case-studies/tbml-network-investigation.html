Trade-Based Money Laundering Network Investigation

Network analytics and PII linkage across ten years of transaction history to uncover a trade-based money laundering scheme involving bulk electronics purchases.

DatabricksPythonSQLNetwork AnalysisCredit Card Analytics

Note: client and institution names are withheld per confidentiality requirements. This engagement is described using generalized role and industry context only.

The Challenge

Outside legal counsel needed support investigating alleged trade-based money laundering involving bulk electronics purchases at a major financial services corporation, working directly alongside the client's financial crime unit to determine whether employees and third-party payors were coordinating suspicious transactions.

The Approach

  • Developed a data ingestion and standardization pipeline consolidating ten years of transaction history for subjects of interest, enabling systematic pattern detection across a large, multi-source dataset.
  • Performed network analytics using PII linkage (name, date of birth, government IDs) to surface previously unknown relationships and connections between employees and third-party payors.
  • Analyzed cardholder transaction behavior across roughly 300 internal employees and 250+ external payment entities, identifying anomalous activity patterns and network clusters indicative of coordinated conduct.
  • Executed time-series and network analytics in Databricks (SQL and Python) to detect behavioral anomalies, map transaction flows, and support enhancements to the client's ongoing transaction monitoring program.
  • Constructed KPI frameworks to quantify fraud detection and monitoring performance, including alert rates, escalation rates, and false-positive ratios across high-volume credit card portfolios.
  • Compiled statistical evidence to substantiate filed Suspicious Activity Reports and corroborate investigative findings, validating results against initial reports and identifying further suspicious conduct.
PII network linkage surfaced connections across 300+ employees and 250+ external payors, corroborating $5B+ in suspicious activity.

The Impact

  • Identified $5 billion or more in fraudulent transactions and $3 billion or more in suspicious payments across the subject population.
  • Corroborated evidence of employee collusion, concentrated bulk electronics and gift card purchases, and anomalous airline ticket spend, directly supporting SAR filings and government and regulator inquiry responses.
  • Uncovered additional network linkages among subjects beyond the original investigation scope, expanding the evidentiary record for outside counsel and regulators.
